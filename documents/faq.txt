DEBT COLLECTION FAQ

Q1: What happens if I can't pay my debt in full?
A: If you cannot pay your debt in full, you have several options. You can request a payment arrangement that allows you to pay in installments over time. We will work with you to create a realistic payment plan based on your financial situation. You may also be eligible for debt counseling through the National Credit Regulator. It's important to contact us as soon as you know you cannot pay - ignoring the debt will not make it go away and may lead to additional fees or legal action.

Q2: Can you call me at work?
A: We may contact you at work only for the purpose of verifying your employment or location. We cannot discuss your debt with your employer or colleagues. If you request that we not contact you at work, we must respect that request and will only contact you at your home or personal phone number. You have the right to request that we stop calling you at your workplace.

Q3: What are my rights under the National Credit Act?
A: Under the National Credit Act, you have several important rights. You have the right to receive written notice of the debt amount and details. You have the right to request verification of the debt within 30 days. You have the right to dispute the debt if you believe it is incorrect. You have the right to request a payment arrangement. You have the right to be treated with dignity and respect. You also have the right to file a complaint with the National Credit Regulator if you believe your rights have been violated.

Q4: How do I know this debt is legitimate?
A: You have the right to request written verification of any debt. Upon request, we must provide you with documentation showing the original creditor, the amount owed, how the amount was calculated, and any fees or interest that have been added. If you believe the debt is not yours or the amount is incorrect, you should dispute it in writing within 30 days of our initial contact. We will investigate and provide evidence of the debt's validity.

Q5: Can you add fees to my debt?
A: We can only add fees that are authorized by law or your original credit agreement. Any fees must be reasonable and must be disclosed to you. Common authorized fees include collection fees, legal fees (if legal action is taken), and interest. We cannot add arbitrary or unauthorized fees. If you believe a fee has been added incorrectly, you have the right to dispute it and request an explanation of how the fee was calculated.

Q6: What if I think the debt collector is harassing me?
A: Debt collection harassment is illegal. Signs of harassment include calling you more than 3 times per day, calling outside of allowed hours (8 AM-8 PM weekdays, 9 AM-1 PM Saturdays), using threatening language, or contacting third parties about your debt. If you believe you are being harassed, document each incident (date, time, what was said) and file a complaint with the National Credit Regulator. You can also request in writing that we stop calling you, though we may still take other legal action to collect the debt.

Q7: How long can you try to collect this debt?
A: In South Africa, most debts become prescribed (legally unenforceable) after 3 years from the date the debt became due, provided you have not acknowledged the debt in writing or made a payment during that time. However, certain types of debts (like mortgage bonds) have longer prescription periods. Acknowledging the debt or making a payment restarts the prescription period. We will inform you if your debt is close to becoming prescribed.

Q8: Can you take legal action against me?
A: We may take legal action if other collection methods have been unsuccessful and the debt remains unpaid. Before taking legal action, we must provide you with notice and an opportunity to pay. Legal action typically involves obtaining a court judgment, which may allow us to garnish your wages or attach your assets. However, legal action is a last resort and we prefer to work out a payment arrangement with you. If you receive legal papers, you should respond within the specified time frame.

Q9: What information do you need from me?
A: We need basic information to verify your identity and locate your account. This may include your full name, ID number, current address, and phone number. We may also need information about your employment and income to assess your ability to pay. You are not required to provide more information than is reasonably necessary. All information you provide is protected under POPIA and will only be used for debt collection purposes.

Q10: How can I stop the collection process?
A: The most effective way to stop the collection process is to pay the debt in full or enter into a payment arrangement that you can maintain. If you believe the debt is not valid, you can dispute it in writing. If you are experiencing financial hardship, you may qualify for debt counseling through a registered debt counselor. Note that simply ignoring our calls or refusing to communicate will not stop the collection process and may lead to escalated collection efforts or legal action.
